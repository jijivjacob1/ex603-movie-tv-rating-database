# Unit 5 Finding: Recommended Actions for At-Risk Movies

To: Catalog Manager and Trust & Safety Lead  
From: Data Analysis  
Re: Actions for movies with frequent rating withdrawals or no ratings

## Recommendations

The data points to two different situations, so these movies should not receive the same response.

**Review Movies 11, 2, and 9 for rating abuse.** These movies have unusually high withdrawal rates among titles with enough ratings to compare: Movie 11 has 5 withdrawals out of 16 ratings (31%), Movie 9 has 4 out of 14 (29%), and Movie 2 has 4 out of 16 (25%). Begin with a human review of the withdrawn ratings and the accounts involved. Movie 2 merits a focused spam check because three of its four withdrawals were for spam. Movie 11 needs review of both the activity and the audience response: it has the highest withdrawal rate and the lowest average score, but removed ratings use a placeholder score that makes the average look worse. Movie 9's withdrawals have several different causes, so review the cases individually rather than assume one pattern. Keep confirmed abuse out of published ratings and report averages using only valid, published ratings. Do not remove or demote a movie based on its unreviewed average alone.

**Investigate Movies 14 and 15 for lack of activity.** Neither has received a rating, despite both being available in the catalog. First confirm that each title is visible and searchable, its details and audience restrictions are correct, and its activity is reaching the ratings system. If those checks pass, give the titles a limited promotion and monitor whether viewers engage. If they remain unrated, review whether their licensing and catalog value justify keeping them. Zero ratings are a reason to investigate discoverability and value, not evidence of poor audience reception or misconduct.

## Decision

Treat frequent withdrawals as a trust-and-quality issue requiring review and moderation. Treat no ratings as a discovery or business-value question requiring validation and a measured promotion decision. This keeps the response proportional to what the evidence actually shows.
